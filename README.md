# Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR)

## 1. Case Overview

This Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR) presents a simulated pharmacovigilance assessment of an adverse drug reaction associated with amoxicillin. The case includes patient information, suspected medicine, adverse reaction, clinical course, causality assessment, seriousness, expectedness, and final assessment.

> **Note:** This is a simulated educational case prepared for pharmacovigilance training purposes.

---

## 2. Patient Information

- **Patient ID:** PV-001
- **Age:** 45 years
- **Sex:** Female
- **Relevant Medical History:** No significant medical history reported
- **Indication for Treatment:** Bacterial respiratory tract infection

---

## 3. Suspected Medicine

- **Drug Name:** Amoxicillin
- **Drug Class:** Penicillin antibiotic
- **Route:** Oral
- **Indication:** Bacterial infection
- **Dose:** 500 mg three times daily
- **Therapy Start:** Day 1
- **Therapy Stop:** Day 4
- **Action Taken:** Amoxicillin discontinued after development of the reaction

---

## 4. Adverse Drug Reaction

### Reported Reaction
**Skin rash and itching**

### Onset
The patient developed a generalized skin rash with itching approximately **3 days after starting amoxicillin**.

### Clinical Course
The patient contacted a healthcare professional after the rash appeared. Amoxicillin was discontinued. The skin rash and itching gradually improved after withdrawal of the suspected medicine.

### Outcome
**Recovered/Improved after discontinuation of amoxicillin.**

---

## 5. ADR Identification

The reported skin rash and itching were identified as a suspected adverse drug reaction associated with amoxicillin.

The following factors support the identification:

1. The reaction occurred after initiation of amoxicillin.
2. The time to onset was clinically compatible with a drug-related reaction.
3. The reaction improved after discontinuation of the suspected drug.
4. Skin rash and itching are recognized reactions associated with amoxicillin.
5. No alternative cause was clearly identified in the available case information.

---

## 6. Seriousness Assessment

### Seriousness: NON-SERIOUS

The reaction did not result in:

- Death
- Life-threatening condition
- Hospitalization
- Prolongation of hospitalization
- Persistent or significant disability
- Congenital anomaly
- Other medically important serious outcome

Therefore, the case is classified as a **non-serious adverse drug reaction**.

---

## 7. Expectedness Assessment

### Expectedness: EXPECTED

Skin rash and itching are recognized adverse reactions associated with amoxicillin and other beta-lactam antibiotics.

Therefore, based on the simulated case information, the reaction is classified as **expected**.

---

## 8. Dechallenge Assessment

### Dechallenge: POSITIVE

Amoxicillin was discontinued following the appearance of the skin rash and itching.

The patient's symptoms subsequently improved.

Therefore, a **positive dechallenge** is present.

---

## 9. Rechallenge Assessment

### Rechallenge: NOT PERFORMED

The patient was not intentionally re-exposed to amoxicillin after recovery.

Therefore, rechallenge information is unavailable.

---

## 10. Causality Assessment

### Causality: PROBABLE

The relationship between amoxicillin and the skin rash/itching is considered **probable** because:

1. There is a reasonable temporal relationship between drug administration and reaction onset.
2. The reaction is consistent with a known adverse reaction to amoxicillin.
3. The symptoms improved after discontinuation of the suspected drug.
4. A positive dechallenge supports the relationship.
5. No rechallenge was performed.
6. Alternative causes cannot be completely excluded.

### Causality Conclusion

**Amoxicillin → Skin rash and itching: PROBABLE**

---

## 11. Severity Assessment

### Severity: MILD TO MODERATE

The reaction was limited to skin rash and itching and improved after discontinuation of the suspected medicine.

There was no evidence of severe systemic involvement in the simulated case.

---

## 12. Final Case Assessment

The patient developed skin rash and itching approximately three days after initiation of amoxicillin. The suspected medicine was discontinued, following which the symptoms improved.

The reaction is classified as:

- **ADR:** Skin rash and itching
- **Seriousness:** Non-Serious
- **Expectedness:** Expected
- **Dechallenge:** Positive
- **Rechallenge:** Not performed
- **Causality:** Probable
- **Severity:** Mild to Moderate
- **Outcome:** Recovered/Improved

Overall, the case demonstrates a **probable and expected non-serious adverse drug reaction associated with amoxicillin**.

---

## 13. Conclusion

Based on the available clinical information, amoxicillin is considered the probable cause of the patient's skin rash and itching. The temporal relationship, known adverse-effect profile, and improvement following drug withdrawal support the causality assessment.


# Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR) – Metformin

## 1. Case Overview

This Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR) presents a simulated pharmacovigilance assessment of a serious adverse drug reaction associated with metformin.

> **Note:** This is a simulated educational case prepared for pharmacovigilance training purposes.

---

## 2. Patient Information

- **Patient ID:** PV-002
- **Age:** 55 years
- **Sex:** Male
- **Medical History:** Type 2 diabetes mellitus
- **Indication for Treatment:** Type 2 diabetes mellitus

---

## 3. Suspected Medicine

- **Drug Name:** Metformin
- **Drug Class:** Biguanide antidiabetic
- **Route:** Oral
- **Indication:** Type 2 diabetes mellitus
- **Dose:** 500 mg twice daily
- **Action Taken:** Metformin discontinued following the adverse reaction

---

## 4. Adverse Drug Reaction

### Reported Reaction
**Lactic acidosis**

### Onset
The patient developed symptoms consistent with lactic acidosis after starting metformin therapy.

### Clinical Course
The patient developed weakness, nausea, rapid breathing, and metabolic abnormalities. Laboratory investigation indicated elevated lactate levels and metabolic acidosis. The patient was admitted to the hospital for urgent medical management.

Metformin was discontinued.

### Outcome
**Recovered following treatment and discontinuation of metformin.**

---

## 5. ADR Identification

Lactic acidosis was identified as a suspected serious adverse drug reaction associated with metformin.

Factors supporting identification include:

1. The reaction occurred during metformin therapy.
2. Lactic acidosis is a recognized serious adverse reaction associated with metformin.
3. Clinical and laboratory findings were consistent with lactic acidosis.
4. Metformin was discontinued following the reaction.
5. The patient received appropriate medical treatment.
6. The patient's condition subsequently improved.

---

## 6. Seriousness Assessment

### Seriousness: SERIOUS

The reaction is classified as **serious** because it resulted in hospitalization and represented a potentially life-threatening clinical condition.

Seriousness criteria include:

- Hospitalization
- Potentially life-threatening condition
- Requirement for urgent medical management

Therefore:

**Seriousness = Serious**

---

## 7. Expectedness Assessment

### Expectedness: EXPECTED

Lactic acidosis is a recognized, rare but serious adverse reaction associated with metformin.

Therefore, based on the simulated case:

**Expectedness = Expected**

---

## 8. Dechallenge Assessment

### Dechallenge: POSITIVE

Metformin was discontinued after the development of lactic acidosis, followed by clinical improvement with appropriate treatment.

Therefore:

**Dechallenge = Positive**

---

## 9. Rechallenge Assessment

### Rechallenge: NOT PERFORMED

The patient was not re-exposed to metformin after the serious adverse reaction.

Therefore:

**Rechallenge = Not performed**

---

## 10. Causality Assessment

### Causality: PROBABLE

The relationship between metformin and lactic acidosis is considered **probable** based on:

1. A compatible temporal relationship between metformin treatment and the reaction.
2. Lactic acidosis is a recognized serious adverse reaction associated with metformin.
3. Metformin was discontinued after the reaction.
4. The patient's condition improved after withdrawal and treatment.
5. Rechallenge was not performed.
6. Other contributing factors should be evaluated before establishing definitive causality.

### Causality Conclusion

**Metformin → Lactic acidosis: PROBABLE**

---

## 11. Severity Assessment

### Severity: SEVERE / LIFE-THREATENING

Lactic acidosis is a potentially life-threatening metabolic disorder requiring urgent medical management and hospitalization.

Therefore:

**Severity = Severe / Potentially Life-Threatening**

---

## 12. Final Case Assessment

The patient developed lactic acidosis during metformin therapy and required hospitalization and urgent medical management. Metformin was discontinued, and the patient subsequently improved.

The reaction is classified as:

- **ADR:** Lactic acidosis
- **Seriousness:** Serious
- **Expectedness:** Expected
- **Dechallenge:** Positive
- **Rechallenge:** Not performed
- **Causality:** Probable
- **Severity:** Severe / Potentially Life-Threatening
- **Outcome:** Recovered
- **Action Taken:** Metformin discontinued

---

## 13. Conclusion

Based on the available simulated case information, metformin is considered a **probable suspected cause** of the lactic acidosis. The reaction is **serious, expected, and potentially life-threatening**.
